The board is the product. The panels beside it serve two jobs at once: a scan surface the operator reads in one second while the run streams, and a detail surface the analyst opens when a number needs its basis. Today both jobs share one long column of 10 pixel text. These concepts split them.

Surface rules. Every component on this page follows them.
  1. Almost nothing is enclosed. Separation comes from alignment, one hairline rule, and space. A border marks an input or a floating surface, nothing else.
  2. One radius. Containers are square. Controls are 2 px. Nothing is rounded past that.
  3. Colour is ink. A state is a text colour, a stroke, or a solid 8 px square. Never a translucent tint over the surface.
  4. No blur, no shadow, no glow. A floating surface is opaque with a 1 px edge. Depth comes from stacking order, not from effects.
  5. Three surfaces. Board, panel, and one sunken tone for an input. The board carries a faint grain so it reads as a material.
  6. Sentence-case labels. Size and colour separate a label from a value. No tracked caps, no condensed cuts.
  7. One emphasis per region. Values are large and regular weight. Titles are medium weight. Bold does not appear in a panel.
  8. Mono for machine data only. Ids, times, coordinates, units and keys. Prose, labels and titles are sans.

02

Palette and contrast

Warm graphite, three surfaces, two rule weights. Colour appears only where it carries meaning: a state, a channel, a threat.

Chrome is monochrome. Buttons, tabs, rules, focus and selection use the neutral ramp. A pressed control inverts to light ink on dark. That leaves red, blue and orange free to mean threat, sensor and effector everywhere.

Why warm. The current sheet and my first draft both used a blue-black ground. Blue-black with matte grey boxes reads as a phone screen. A warm graphite with a faint grain reads as a surface, and it does not fight the sensor blue.

03

Controls, state, marks

Buttons are 28 px at 2 px radius and come in four weights. A pressed control inverts. State is a solid square and a word. Provenance is a mark, not a pill.

What looked improvised before. Every tag on the panel was the same bordered mono box: PF, SRC, DOC, PROB, PASS, FAIL, LIMITS, ENGAGED, TRACK, READY. Basis, status and phase all shared one form, so none of them read at a glance.

04

Analysis rail

The right panel becomes three layers. A pinned scan strip that never scrolls. A rail of one-line widget rows that each carry their headline figure. A focus drawer on the board for the one widget that needs width.

Rules the rail enforces.
Every widget row shows its headline figure while closed.
Provenance is stated once per widget, in the row, not once per tile.
Caveats are counted in the row and listed once in the drawer, never repeated per card.
Pin sends a figure to the scan strip. Focus sends the widget to the drawer.

What changed and why

  1. Scan strip. Four pinned figures and the run clock stay on screen at every scroll depth. The operator reads live state without hunting. Pins are per user and per tab.
  2. Rows carry their headline. A closed widget is no longer an empty caps title. It shows the one figure or state you would open it for, so most widgets stay closed most of the time.
  3. Provenance once per widget. "Planning envelope" moved from six tile captions into one row caption and one mark in the footer. Tile captions now carry a fact the tile alone owns.
  4. Gates are rows, not cards. Six gates fit in 200 px with a square, a value and a chevron. The eighteen repeated caveat sentences became one count and one list.
  5. Sub-tabs filter the rail. Run, Sensors, Effectors, System and Threat show a subset of the same twelve widgets. A widget can sit in two tabs. "Customize" becomes a count that opens the picker.
  6. Hover reveals actions. Pin and focus appear on hover or when open, so the row stays clean while the run streams.
Try it. Click a row title to open or close it. Use the density control in section 01. Press Focus or Open on board on Requirement gates to see the drawer in section 05.
05

Library dock and focus drawer

The library leaves the layout. A 52 px dock holds one glyph per category. A category opens an opaque flyout over the board, and the flyout closes on drop or on Escape. Search is the first control, and ⌘K opens it from anywhere.

The board never reflows. The flyout and the drawer sit over the canvas. The dock replaces 230 px of permanent column with 52 px.

The drawer is where a widget goes when it needs width: a chart at full run length, a gate table with its caveats beside it, an event ledger. One at a time, closes with Escape.

The scrubber carries event ticks: detections in grey, shots in orange. The engagement timeline is no longer a collapsed strip.

Inspector

The same rail rules, applied to a selected entity. A sticky sub-nav replaces one long scroll. Values sit in a key and value grid with the provenance mark in a fixed third column.

What changed and why

  1. Header holds identity and state. Name, type, role and the live state stay visible. The name field moves to a rename action, since renaming is rare and reading is constant.
  2. Sub-nav instead of one scroll. The current Inspector runs Identity, Position, Performance, Rings, Network and Notes end to end. A tab row with counts lets the analyst jump.
  3. Key, value, mark. Three fixed columns. Values are mono and right aligned so units line up. The mark column is 36 px and always present, so its absence also means something: a fact with no basis to state.
  4. Editable is visible. Only fields that accept input carry an input frame. Read-only values sit flat. Today every value looks like a control or none does.
  5. Explanations under the row. The mount height note stays, at 12 px secondary, under the grid it explains, instead of interrupting the grid.
07

Foundation and function

Ten changes below the skin. Each one was found in the source, not in a screenshot. The first three come before any panel work.

Evidence. The timeline advances the store on every animation frame and the Inspector subscribes to the whole scenario. The T&E Ops surface carries a second kit of 33 components. The stylesheet has no light theme. Panels carry 11 aria labels in total. The layer state has 17 booleans. Persistence is one localStorage key with no version.

08

Order of work

Each step stands alone and ships alone. Foundations first, then the two stylesheet steps that change every panel at once, then the panels, then the board.

Reach says how much of the product a step touches. Every panel and App steps go first because later steps build on them.
